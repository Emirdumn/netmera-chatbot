What is Segmentation?
 

Segmentation is the process of grouping potential customers by 
similar characteristics
 such as age, gender, interests, and preferences.
Segmentation is the process of breaking down a larger group of people, clients, or target audience into more manageable, distinct subgroups according to predetermined traits, behaviors, or demographics. The goal of segmentation is to develop more targeted and customized marketing strategies, messages, and offerings that better speak to the particular needs and preferences of each subgroup.
Businesses can deliver more relevant and individualized experiences thanks to segmentation, increasing the chance of engagement and conversion. By avoiding a “one-size-fits-all” strategy, it enables marketers to focus their efforts on the unique needs, passions, and problems of each segment.
As it ensures that resources are allocated to the most promising opportunities and that messaging speaks directly to the desires of each segment, segmentation is a fundamental step in developing effective marketing strategies. 
 

Examples of Different Segmentation Types

 

Demographics:
 Characteristics such as age, gender, income, education, occupation, and marital status.
Geographics:
 Geographic location, such as country, city, region, or climate.
Psychographics:
 
Lifestyle, values, interests, hobbies, attitudes, and behaviors.
Behavioral:
 
Purchase history, brand interactions, website activity, and usage patterns.
Purchase Intent:
 
The likelihood of making a purchase in the near future.
B2B Segmentation:
 
Factors like industry, company size, job title, and buying behavior.
Occasion:
 
Segmenting based on specific occasions or events, like holidays or special promotions.
